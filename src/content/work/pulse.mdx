## The job

I owned mobile for the Pulse enterprise inspection platform, Android and iOS end to end, and led a team of
4–6 mobile engineers. I architected the mobile engine from an early prototype to **100+ B2B enterprise
clients** and **20,000+ field users in 10+ countries**.

Clients during my time included Accor (Pullman, Mövenpick), Tim Hortons, Rebel Foods, Travel Food Services,
Leica, Battery Smart, Fichtner, Nippon Logistics and a Middle Eastern royal commission. The Android app is
rated **4.7★ from 3,300+ reviews** on Google Play.

## A sync contract first

I designed the sync contract with the backend and web teams: payloads, upload
semantics and failure behaviour. Then I implemented both native clients against it.

## Built for auditors without signal

Auditors inspect sites with no connectivity, so the data layer is offline-first:

- a **dynamic form builder** for inspection forms
- a **background media upload queue**
- a **local SQLite cache**

The engine handled real inspections of **500+ questions with 1,000+ photos**, captured offline and uploaded
in the background once a connection came back.

**No data-loss incidents were reported in 4.5 years of field use.**

## Two native clients, kept in parity

I wrote and shipped the Swift/iOS client to the App Store alongside Android, with feature parity between
the two. The field UIs are usable by non-technical staff with no training.

## One codebase: the React Native rewrite

In 2025 I led a full rewrite of both native apps into a single **React Native** codebase. I architected it
and built it alongside the team, and we completed it in six months. It replaced both native apps in
production.

## Releases that stopped being events

I introduced the release process with GitHub Actions and Fastlane: a predictable bi-weekly cadence and
same-day hotfixes, replacing manual builds.
